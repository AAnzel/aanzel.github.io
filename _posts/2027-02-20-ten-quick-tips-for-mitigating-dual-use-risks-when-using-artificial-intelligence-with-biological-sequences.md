This paper examines dual-use research of concern (DURC) in the context of rapidly advancing biological artificial intelligence (AI), with a focus on genomics, proteomics, and adjacent fields. We argue that biological-sequence models, design tools, and large language models integrated with laboratory automation simultaneously accelerate discovery and lower barriers to misuse. This challenges existing bio-risk governance approaches, although the magnitude and operational relevance of this effect remain contested. Drawing on international DURC and biosecurity frameworks, we adapt a three-phase institutional risk-assessment model --- spanning identification, risk–benefit analysis, and iterative review and decision --- to AI-enabled life science and biomedical research. On this basis, we propose ten practical tips that translate high-level dual-use concerns into concrete steps for individual researchers, groups, and institutions, aiming to embed proactive DURC assessment, legal and regulatory compliance, and self-governance into the use of AI in high-stakes biological domains while preserving legitimate scientific progress.
